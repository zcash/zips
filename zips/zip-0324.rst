Terminology
===========

The key words "MUST", "MUST NOT", "SHOULD", "SHOULD NOT", "RECOMMENDED", and
"MAY" in this document are to be interpreted as described in BCP 14 [#BCP14]_
when, and only when, they appear in all capitals.

Zcash protocol terms are as defined in the Zcash Protocol Specification.
[#protocol]_

The terms "Mainnet" and "Testnet" in this document are to be interpreted as
defined in the Zcash protocol specification [#protocol-networks]_. "Regtest"
refers to the regression-test network used by Zcash full node and wallet
implementations for local testing.

.. _applink:

By “applink” we mean a platform mechanism for triggering local applications
using HTTPS links, such as App Links [#app-links]_ on Android, Universal Links
[#universal-links]_ on iOS, or App URI handlers [#app-uri-handlers]_ on Windows.

The terms below are to be interpreted as follows:

URI-Encapsulated Payment (or *liberated payment*)
  An amount of ZEC that is held by a *payment wallet* whose sole spending
  secret is carried inside a URI, so that whoever holds the URI can take the
  funds. "Liberated payment" is a product-neutral synonym used in user-facing
  contexts; the two terms are interchangeable in this document.
Payment-Encapsulating URI (or *payment URI*)
  The URI that carries a URI-Encapsulated Payment, as specified in `Syntax`_.
Payment secret
  The 32-byte secret $S$ carried in the ``key`` parameter of a payment URI,
  from which every key of the payment wallet is derived.
Payment wallet
  The single-account wallet derived from a payment secret as specified in
  `Payment wallet derivation`_. Its Sapling funds, if any, are outside the
  scope of this specification.
Payment address
  The Orchard-only payment address of the payment wallet to which the funds of
  a URI-Encapsulated Payment are sent.
Minter
  The party (and their wallet software) that creates a payment URI and funds
  its payment address. This is the *sender* of the payment.
Claimant
  The party (and their wallet software) that uses a payment URI to move the
  funds held by the payment wallet into a wallet of their own. In the usual
  case this is the intended *recipient* of the payment.
Funding transaction
  A transaction that sends funds to the payment address.
Claim transaction
  A transaction, created by a claimant, that spends all of the spendable notes
  held by a payment wallet to one of the claimant's own addresses.
Reclaim
  A claim transaction created by the minter, taking back funds that were not
  claimed by anyone else.


Abstract
========

This proposal specifies a mechanism for sending Zcash payments via any
unmodified (secure) messaging service, printed medium, or other channel over
which a short string can be conveyed. The sender need not know the recipient's
address and the recipient need not have a Zcash wallet already installed.

A payment is created by deriving a fresh 32-byte *payment secret*, deriving a
*payment wallet* from that secret, and sending funds to its Orchard-only
payment address. The payment secret, together with a block height from which
the funds can be found and optional advisory metadata (amount and description),
is encoded in the fragment of an ordinary HTTPS URL. Because the fragment is
never sent to the server named by the URL, the host serves only as an applink_
target for wallet software and as a landing page for recipients without a
compatible wallet.

Anyone who learns the URI can claim the payment: their wallet derives the
payment wallet, scans the chain from the given height, and sweeps every note it
finds into an address of its own in a single transaction, taking the whole
balance rather than the advertised amount. Until that happens the minter can
take the funds back by the same procedure; there is no on-chain expiry and no
limit on the age of a payment. A claim transaction is an ordinary
fully-shielded Orchard transaction,
indistinguishable on chain from any other, and it is constructed so that the
payment secret does not grant visibility of where the funds went after the
claim.

The proposal specifies the syntax and semantics of Payment-Encapsulating URIs,
the derivation of payment wallets, the workflow of minting, claiming and
reclaiming payments, and the behaviour expected of wallet software and of
hosts that appear in payment URIs. It also describes, informatively, the
payment-link formats already deployed by the Vizor wallet, which follow the
same model of a bearer secret for a standard single-account wallet.

Usage Story
-----------

Alice wants to send a 1.23 ZEC payment to Bob. She doesn't have Bob's Zcash
address — and Bob may not even have a wallet or have heard of Zcash! However,
she does have some end-to-end encrypted channel to Bob on a secure messaging
app, such as Signal or WhatsApp. She instructs her own Zcash wallet to send
1.23 ZEC on that channel. Her wallet derives a new payment secret from her
wallet seed, sends 1.2301 ZEC to the corresponding payment address, and
constructs a payment URI containing the payment secret, which she sends to Bob
via the secure messaging app.

Bob receives the message, which looks as follows:

    This message contains a Zcash payment.
    Click the following link to view it and receive the funds using your Zcash
    wallet app:

    https://example.com/#v=1&key=zgift1...&height=3400000&amount=1.23&desc=Payment%20for%20foo

    If you do not yet have a Zcash wallet app, see: https://z.cash/wallets

Bob clicks the link. His Zcash mobile wallet app (which is already installed
and has configured itself as an applink_ handler for ``example.com``) shows up
and tells Bob that a payment of about 1.23 ZEC awaits him. The wallet app
scans the block chain from block 3400000 for the funds, confirms that they are
present and unspent, and offers to claim the payment. Bob clicks the “Claim”
button, and his wallet app generates a transaction moving the money to his own
address (using the extra 0.0001 ZEC he has received to pay the transaction
fee). When this transaction is confirmed on chain the payment is final, and
Bob can send the funds to other parties.

If Bob had no compatible wallet installed, following the link would instead
have shown him a static web page at ``example.com`` explaining what the link
is and where to get a wallet; the page never learns the payment secret, which
stays in the fragment on Bob's device.

The same mechanism serves "gift card" style uses: a merchant or event
organiser mints a batch of payments, prints each URI as a QR code, and funds
each card once. Whoever receives a card claims it; cards that are never
claimed are reclaimed by the minter after an announced date.


Motivation
==========

This proposal enables sending of funds without exposing users to the notion of
payment addresses and their secure distribution. Instead, funds can be sent
using any pre-existing communication channel, by a single message sent
unidirectionally from the sender to the recipient. This message conveys all
the information needed to obtain control of the funds, compactly expressed as
a textual URI.

Consequently, all functionality related to contact discovery and secure-channel
establishment can be delegated to the message app(s) with which the user is
already familiar, and in which the user has already established communication
channels to many of their contacts.

Moreover, funds can be sent to users who have not yet installed wallet software
and who do not yet have a payment address. The recipient can collect the funds
at any later time by installing suitable software. This also makes the
mechanism suitable for onboarding: a payment URI can be printed, handed out at
an event, or embedded in a gift card, and the recipient's first interaction
with Zcash can be receiving money rather than generating an address.

Finally, this avoids the fat-finger problem of sending funds to the wrong
address. The user sends funds via a known contact on, e.g., WhatsApp, who they
have a relationship with or at least can confirm the recipient's identity.
Moreover, even once funds are sent via a URI, they can be recovered if the
other party does not claim them.

The proposal is complementary to ZIP 321 [#zip-0321]_, which standardizes
*Payment Request URIs* with which the payment *recipient* conveys their
persistent payment address to the *sender*, for subsequent fund transfers (to
be done using the normal on-chain mechanism rather than the encapsulated
payments described in the current proposal).


Privacy Implications
====================

A URI-Encapsulated Payment involves two on-chain transactions: a funding
transaction from the minter to the payment address, and a claim transaction
from the payment address to the claimant. Both are fully-shielded Orchard
transactions and are not distinguishable on chain from other Orchard
transactions, except through timing correlation and the statistics of their
numbers of actions.

The holder of a payment URI holds the payment wallet's full viewing key. They
can therefore see the funding transaction(s) and the fact that the notes were
spent. The claim transaction is specified so that its output is *not*
decryptable with the payment wallet's outgoing viewing key; consequently a
party who later obtains the URI (for example, from a chat backup or a
discarded printed card) learns that the payment was claimed, but not the
claimant's address or any other details of the claim output. The trade-off is
that the claimant's own wallet is the only record of where the funds went.

The minter learns when (and that) a payment was claimed, because the notes it
can view become spent. The ``amount`` and ``desc`` parameters are readable by
anyone who sees the URI, including in retrospect.

The host named in a payment URI never receives the payment secret in an HTTP
request, because it is carried in the fragment. However, any script served by
that host *can* read the fragment, so the host operator (or anyone who can
modify its content) is in a position to steal unclaimed funds if the landing
page is not static. Wallet software that handles the URI through an applink_
avoids any request to the host at all.


Requirements
============

This proposal's specification of URI-Encapsulated Payments, and the intended
protocols for using them, is meant to fulfill the following requirements:

* The protocol must not require the sender of a payment to stay online until
  the recipient receives the URI (let alone claims the payment).

* URIs should be short enough to render conveniently, to fit in a QR code of
  modest size, and to be compatible with length-limited messaging platforms.

* It must not be feasible for someone who has not seen the URI (or has
  compromised a party who has) to collect the funds.

* No server, indexer, or other third party should need to learn the payment
  secret, and claiming should be possible using only the ordinary light-client
  infrastructure that a shielded wallet already depends on.

* The URIs and protocol should minimize the likelihood of inadvertent misuse,
  and in particular the risks discussed in `Security Considerations`_ below.

* The protocol must not leak any information (sender identity, recipient
  identity, amount, description) to third parties, other than inevitable
  metadata about the existence of a transaction, the inevitable network
  communication around sending/receipt of transactions, whatever leakage is
  induced by the communication channel used to transmit the URI, and whatever
  is voluntarily shared by the parties.

* The URIs should allow for future modifications and expansion of the format,
  without risk of ambiguous parsing.

* The on-chain footprint of payments that use this mechanism should be
  indistinguishable from normal fully-shielded transactions (except, possibly,
  for the statistics of the number of shielded inputs and outputs).

* A payment should remain claimable for as long as its funds are unspent, so
  that a payment that is printed, kept, or opened late does not silently
  become unclaimable.

* Don't lose funds, even if wallets crash, or everything but the sending
  wallet master secret is lost. Payments minted by a wallet should be
  recoverable from that wallet's seed, and a payment URI on its own should be
  recoverable by any wallet that can restore a standard mnemonic phrase.

Non-requirements
================

* It is outside the scope of this proposal to establish a secure communication
  channel for transmission of URI-Encapsulated Payments, or to protect the
  parties' devices from security compromise.

* Claiming the payment may involve significant wait times, on the scale of
  minutes, as the requisite on-chain transactions are generated, mined and
  confirmed, and as the claimant's wallet scans the chain from the given
  height. This proposal does not try to solve this (though it does try to
  avoid imposing significant additional delays, and it does address how the
  intermediate state is conveyed to the user).

* A URI-Encapsulated Payment is not a long-term store of value and is not a
  substitute for a wallet backup. See `Security Considerations`_.

* This proposal does not provide an on-chain expiry for unclaimed payments.
  Reclaiming is a wallet-side action of the minter.

* Funds sent to Sapling addresses of the payment wallet are outside the scope
  of this proposal. Such funds can be recovered only by restoring the payment
  wallet's mnemonic sentence in a wallet that supports Sapling.


Specification
=============

A Payment-Encapsulating URI represents the capability to claim whatever
spendable funds are held, now or later, by the payment wallet derived from the
payment secret it carries. See `Usage Story`_ for an example.

Syntax
------

A Payment-Encapsulating URI is a Uniform Resource Locator (URL), as defined in
RFC 3986 [#RFC3986]_, with the following structure::

  payment-uri  = "https://" host [ ":" port ] path-abempty "#" fragment
  fragment     = param *( "&" param )
  param        = version / keyparam / heightparam / amountparam / descparam
               / otherparam
  version      = "v=1"
  keyparam     = "key=" bech32m
  heightparam  = "height=" %x31-39 *DIGIT
  amountparam  = "amount=" 1*DIGIT [ "." 1*8DIGIT ]
  descparam    = "desc=" *qchar
  otherparam   = 1*( ALPHA / DIGIT / "-" / "_" ) "=" *qchar
  qchar        = unreserved / pct-encoded / "+"

where ``host``, ``port``, ``path-abempty``, ``unreserved``, ``pct-encoded``,
``ALPHA``, and ``DIGIT`` are as defined in RFC 3986 [#RFC3986]_, and
``bech32m`` is a Bech32m string as specified in `Encoding of the payment
secret`_. This grammar describes the URIs that producers emit; the handling of
other input by consumers is specified below.

The scheme MUST be ``https``; consumers MUST match it case-insensitively, as
RFC 3986 requires. The ``host`` MAY be any host; see `Hosts and landing
pages`_. The path SHOULD be empty or ``/``. Producers MUST NOT include a query
component (``?``): everything specific to the payment MUST be carried in the
fragment, so that it is never sent to the host. Consumers MUST NOT use a query
component, if one is present, to interpret the payment.

The fragment consists of attribute-value pairs separated by ``&``. The
following attributes are defined:

* ``v``: the format version. MUST be present and MUST have the value ``1`` for
  URIs conforming to this version of this specification. A consumer that
  encounters any other value MUST reject the URI as unsupported.
* ``key``: the payment secret, encoded as specified in `Encoding of the
  payment secret`_. MUST be present.
* ``height``: a block height, in decimal without sign, whitespace or leading
  zeros, at or before the height of the first funding transaction, and no less
  than the minimum birthday height given in `Semantics`_. MUST be present.
* ``amount``: the nominal amount of the payment in decimal ZEC, following the
  same syntax and interpretation as the ``amount`` parameter of ZIP 321
  [#zip-0321-amount]_; in addition, it MUST be greater than zero. MAY be
  present. This value is advisory; see `Semantics`_.
* ``desc``: a human-readable description of the payment. MAY be present. The
  value is UTF-8 text [#RFC3629]_, percent-encoded as described below, and
  MUST NOT exceed 512 bytes after decoding.

The attributes ``v``, ``key`` and ``height`` are *strict*, and ``amount`` and
``desc`` are *advisory*.

Producers MUST emit ``v`` first, followed by ``key``, ``height``, ``amount``
(if present) and ``desc`` (if present), in that order. Consumers MUST accept
the defined attributes in any order.

Consumers process the fragment as follows:

* Empty pairs (as produced by ``&&``, or by a leading or trailing ``&``) MUST
  be skipped.
* A pair that contains no ``=`` is read as a name with an empty value.
* Pairs whose name is not a defined attribute MUST be ignored, whether or not
  they contain ``=``, so that future versions of this specification can add
  optional attributes without changing ``v``.
* Consumers MUST reject a URI in which a strict attribute is absent, appears
  more than once, or is malformed or out of range.
* If an advisory attribute appears more than once, or its value is malformed
  or out of range (for ``amount``: not of the syntax above, zero, or greater
  than the maximum money supply; for ``desc``: not decodable as described
  below, or longer than 512 bytes after decoding), consumers MUST treat that
  attribute as absent and MUST NOT reject the URI for that reason.

Producers MUST percent-encode every byte of the ``desc`` value other than the
``unreserved`` characters of RFC 3986 (``ALPHA``, ``DIGIT``, ``-``, ``.``,
``_``, ``~``); in particular a space MUST be encoded as ``%20``. Consumers
MUST decode ``%XX`` sequences and MUST treat an unencoded ``+`` as a space (for
compatibility with form-encoding libraries). A ``desc`` value that contains a
``%`` not followed by two hexadecimal digits, or that does not decode to valid
UTF-8, is malformed.

A Payment-Encapsulating URI MUST NOT exceed 16384 bytes. Consumers MAY reject
longer inputs, measured after removing leading and trailing whitespace, before
parsing them.

Rationale for advisory attributes being lenient
'''''''''''''''''''''''''''''''''''''''''''''''

The funds held by the payment wallet are authoritative, and ``amount`` and
``desc`` only describe them. Rejecting a URI because one of them is malformed
would make a funded payment unclaimable in conforming wallets because of a
display-only field, which is the worse failure for a bearer instrument that
may already be printed. The strict attributes, by contrast, determine which
wallet is scanned and from where; guessing at them could hide funds or point
the claimant at the wrong wallet.

   </details>

Sanitization of the description
'''''''''''''''''''''''''''''''

The ``desc`` value is text chosen by whoever produced the URI. Before
displaying it, or using it in any other way (for example in a memo), a
consumer MUST transform the decoded value as follows:

* each line break, paragraph separator or other whitespace control character
  (TAB, LF, VT, FF, CR, NEL U+0085, LINE SEPARATOR U+2028 and PARAGRAPH
  SEPARATOR U+2029) is replaced by a space;
* every other control character (Unicode general category ``Cc``, that is the
  C0 controls, DEL and the C1 controls) is removed;
* the invisible format characters that can hide or reorder displayed text are
  removed: the bidirectional marks (U+061C, U+200E, U+200F), the bidirectional
  embeddings, overrides and isolates (U+202A to U+202E and U+2066 to U+2069),
  the deprecated format controls (U+206A to U+206F), ZERO WIDTH SPACE
  (U+200B), WORD JOINER and the invisible operators (U+2060 to U+2064), ZERO
  WIDTH NO-BREAK SPACE (U+FEFF), and the interlinear annotation controls
  (U+FFF9 to U+FFFB).

ZERO WIDTH JOINER, ZERO WIDTH NON-JOINER and variation selectors are kept,
since emoji sequences and several scripts depend on them. If the result is
empty or consists only of whitespace, ``desc`` is treated as absent.

Producers MUST NOT emit a ``desc`` value that is blank, or that this
transformation would change.

Semantics
---------

The ``key`` parameter deterministically determines a payment wallet, as
specified in `Payment wallet derivation`_. The URI represents the capability to
spend every Orchard note (and, once the Ironwood pool is deployed, every
Ironwood note) received by that wallet.

The ``height`` parameter is the *birthday* of the payment wallet: the block
height from which a consumer scans the chain for the payment wallet's notes.
It MUST be less than or equal to the height of the block containing the first
funding transaction for the payment address. A producer SHOULD set it as close
to that height as it can determine at the time the URI is generated (for
example, the chain tip height when the funding transaction is created), since
a smaller value only increases the claimant's scanning cost. A consumer MUST
NOT assume that no notes exist below ``height``; it simply does not look for
them.

``height`` MUST be greater than or equal to the *minimum birthday height* of
the network identified by ``key``: the activation height of NU5, which is
1687104 on Mainnet and 1842420 on Testnet, or 1 on Regtest. Consumers MUST
reject a URI whose ``height`` is lower. A payment wallet's Orchard funds
cannot predate the activation of NU5, which introduced the Orchard pool.
There is no upper bound and no lower bound relative to the chain tip; see
`Payment Rendering and Blockchain Lookup`_.

The ``amount`` and ``desc`` parameters are advisory. They exist so that wallet
software can describe the payment to the user before the chain has been
scanned. The amount that can actually be claimed is the total value of the
unspent notes held by the payment wallet, less the transaction fee
of the claim transaction, and MAY differ from ``amount`` in either direction
(possibly being zero).

The recipient's wallet software SHOULD convey to the user that the ``amount``
and ``desc`` values are merely claims made by the party who sent the URI, and
may be tentative, inaccurate or malicious.

In particular, the recipient's wallet software SHOULD convey to the user that
the amount of ZEC they can successfully transfer to their wallet may be
different than that given by the ``amount`` parameter, and may change
(possibly to zero), until the claim transaction has been mined.

The payment associated with a URI is not deemed “received” by the recipient
until a claim transaction has been mined; see `Claiming`_.

Encoding of the payment secret
------------------------------

The payment secret $S$ is a sequence of exactly 32 bytes. It is encoded in the
``key`` parameter using Bech32m [#bip-0350]_, with the 32 bytes converted to
5-bit groups as for a Bech32 data part [#zip-0173]_ and with the following
Human-Readable Parts:

============== =====================
Network        Human-Readable Part
============== =====================
Mainnet        ``zgift``
Testnet        ``zgifttest``
Regtest        ``zgiftregtest``
============== =====================

A Bech32m-encoded payment secret is 52 data characters plus the 6-character
checksum, the ``1`` separator, and the Human-Readable Part.

Producers MUST use lowercase. Consumers MUST accept either all-lowercase or
all-uppercase encodings (so the Human-Readable Part is matched
case-insensitively), as for Bech32, and MUST reject:

* a string whose checksum is not a valid *Bech32m* checksum (in particular a
  string carrying a Bech32 checksum MUST be rejected);
* a string whose decoded data part is not exactly 32 bytes;
* a string whose padding bits (the 4 bits left over when the 52 5-bit groups
  are converted to 32 bytes) are not all zero, since these would give a second
  spelling of the same secret;
* a string that mixes lowercase and uppercase characters;
* a string whose Human-Readable Part is not one of those listed above
  ("unsupported network");
* a string whose Human-Readable Part identifies a network other than the one
  the consuming wallet is operating on ("wrong network"). A wallet MUST NOT
  derive or display a payment address for a payment secret on the wrong
  network, and SHOULD tell the user which network the URI is for.

.. note::
    The Human-Readable Parts above are specific to this ZIP. If a registry of
    Bech32/Bech32m Human-Readable Parts used by Zcash is established (for
    example in ZIP 173 [#zip-0173]_ or ZIP 316 [#zip-0316]_), the entries
    ``zgift``, ``zgifttest`` and ``zgiftregtest`` are to be registered for
    "ZIP 324 payment secret" on Mainnet, Testnet and Regtest respectively.

Payment wallet derivation
-------------------------

Given a payment secret $S$ and a network, the payment wallet is derived as
follows:

1. Let $\mathsf{mnemonic}$ be the BIP 39 [#bip-0039]_ mnemonic sentence
   obtained from the 256 bits of entropy $S$ using the English word list. This
   is a 24-word sentence whose final 8 bits are the BIP 39 checksum of $S$.
2. Let $\mathsf{seed}$ be the 64-byte BIP 39 seed derived from
   $\mathsf{mnemonic}$ with an empty passphrase (that is, with the PBKDF2 salt
   ``"mnemonic"``).
3. Let $\mathit{coin\_type}$ be the SLIP 44 [#slip-0044]_ coin type for the
   network: $133$ for Mainnet and $1$ for Testnet and Regtest.
4. Let $\mathsf{sk}$ be the Orchard spending key at ZIP 32 path
   $m_\mathsf{Orchard} / 32' / \mathit{coin\_type}' / 0'$ derived from
   $\mathsf{seed}$ as specified in ZIP 32 [#zip-0032-orchard]_ (that is,
   account $0$ of the seed).
5. The *payment address* is the Orchard diversified payment address of
   $\mathsf{sk}$ at diversifier index $0$ [#protocol-orchardkeycomponents]_.
   When the payment address needs to be encoded, it MUST be encoded as a
   Unified Address [#zip-0316]_ containing only that Orchard Receiver.

The Orchard full viewing key, incoming viewing key and outgoing viewing key of
the payment wallet are those of $\mathsf{sk}$.

The pools within the scope of this specification are the Orchard pool and,
once it is deployed, the Ironwood pool [#zip-0258]_. A minter MUST fund the
payment address, that is the Orchard receiver, or the receiver in a later
shielded pool such as Ironwood to which a payment to the payment address is
directed once that pool is deployed. A minter MUST NOT send funds to any
Sapling or transparent address derived from $\mathsf{seed}$; no transparent
receiver is ever advertised for a payment wallet.

A claimant scans for and spends the payment wallet's Orchard notes and, once
the Ironwood pool is deployed, its Ironwood notes. Transparent value can
reach a payment wallet only if someone pays one of its transparent addresses
(the BIP 44 [#bip-0044]_ addresses of account $0$ of $\mathsf{seed}$ for
$\mathit{coin\_type}$) out of band; a claimant that finds such funds MUST
sweep them as specified in `Claiming`_. Funds sent to a Sapling receiver of
the payment wallet are outside the scope of this specification: they are
recoverable only by restoring the mnemonic sentence of step 1 in a wallet that
supports Sapling (see also `Known Uses`_).

Implementations MAY compute $\mathsf{seed}$ directly from $S$ without
materialising the mnemonic sentence, provided the result is identical.

Rationale for an Orchard-only payment wallet
''''''''''''''''''''''''''''''''''''''''''''

.. raw:: html

   <details>
   <summary>Click to show/hide</summary>

Funding only the current shielded pool keeps the usual claim transaction free
of cross-pool transfers, which would be visible on chain as
changes in pool balances and would link the funding and claim transactions.
Orchard is chosen because it is the pool with the best privacy properties,
because its proofs are the cheapest to generate on mobile devices, and
because scanning for Orchard notes is the smallest workload for a light
client. Supporting older pools would multiply the scanning cost for every
claimant for no benefit to the minter, who controls which pool the funds are
sent to. The Ironwood pool is included because it is the successor to
Orchard; a payment funded after its deployment should not be stranded because
the sender's wallet directed it there.

Some deployed payment links (see `Known Uses`_) describe a payment address
with both Orchard and Sapling receivers. Funds that such a link's minter sent
to the Sapling receiver are not claimable under this specification, but
remain recoverable from the mnemonic sentence in any wallet that supports
Sapling.

.. raw:: html

   </details>

Rationale for deriving the payment wallet through a BIP 39 mnemonic
''''''''''''''''''''''''''''''''''''''''''''''''''''''''''''''''''''

.. raw:: html

   <details>
   <summary>Click to show/hide</summary>

Deriving the ZIP 32 seed via a BIP 39 mnemonic rather than directly from $S$
means that the payment wallet is an ordinary single-account wallet: any Zcash
wallet that can restore a 24-word phrase can recover the funds of a payment
URI, even if it has no support for this ZIP, by restoring the phrase and
scanning from the birthday. This provides a recovery path independent of any
particular software and lets a wallet "upgrade" a payment into a persistent
wallet if it chooses. The cost is one PBKDF2 derivation of 2048 iterations,
which is negligible.

.. raw:: html

   </details>

Minting a payment secret
------------------------

A payment secret MUST have 256 bits of entropy. It is obtained in one of two
ways.

**Seed-derived secrets.** A wallet that mints payments from a ZIP 32 seed $S_w$
(the wallet's own seed, not to be confused with the payment secret) MUST derive
payment secrets using ZIP 32 registered key derivation
[#zip-0032-registered]_, with:

- $\mathsf{ContextString} = \texttt{“Zcash\_URIEncapsulatedPayment”}$
- $\mathsf{ZipNumber} = 324$

The payment secret with index $\mathit{index}$ on the network with SLIP 44
coin type $\mathit{coin\_type}$ is the 32-byte key component of

$\mathsf{CKDreg}(\mathsf{CKDreg}(\mathsf{RegKD}(\texttt{“Zcash\_URIEncapsulatedPayment”}, S_w, 324), \mathit{coin\_type} + 2^{31}, [\,]), \mathit{index} + 2^{31}, [\,])$

or, in path notation, $m_{324} / 324' / \mathit{coin\_type}' / \mathit{index}'$,
where $m_{324}$ is the registered subtree root and the chain code of the
resulting child is discarded.

$\mathit{index}$ MUST be in the range $\{ 0\,..\, 2^{31} - 1 \}$. A minting
wallet MUST NOT reuse an index for which it has previously produced a URI, and
SHOULD allocate indices sequentially starting from $0$ so that payments can be
recovered as described in `Recovery From Wallet Crash`_.

**Random secrets.** A minter that does not derive payments from a seed (for
example a service that mints payments in bulk) MAY instead generate $S$
uniformly at random using a cryptographically secure random number generator.
Such a minter is responsible for its own record of outstanding secrets if it
intends to reclaim payments.

Hosts and landing pages
-----------------------

The ``host`` of a Payment-Encapsulating URI serves two purposes: it is the
domain against which wallet software registers an applink_ handler, and it
serves a *landing page* to users whose device has no wallet that handles the
URI.

A wallet that supports this ZIP SHOULD register as an applink_ handler for the
hosts whose payment URIs it intends to handle, and MUST also accept a payment
URI pasted or shared into it directly, regardless of host, provided that the
URI conforms to `Syntax`_.

The operator of a host that appears in payment URIs:

* MUST serve the association files required by the applink_ mechanisms of the
  platforms it supports, listing the wallet applications that may handle its
  URIs, and SHOULD publish the policy under which applications are listed;
* MUST NOT serve, at the path used by payment URIs, any script or other
  active content that reads the fragment identifier, transmits it, or
  stores it — including analytics, error-reporting and tag-management
  scripts;
* SHOULD serve a static page that explains that the link carries a Zcash
  payment, that the funds can be taken by anyone who has the link, and how to
  obtain a compatible wallet;
* SHOULD serve the page with HTTP headers that prevent the fragment from
  leaking through referrers (for example a ``Referrer-Policy`` of
  ``no-referrer``) and that restrict script execution (for example a
  ``Content-Security-Policy`` without ``script-src``).

A wallet that opens a payment URI through an applink_ does not contact the
host at all. A wallet MUST NOT fetch the URI over the network itself.

Testing
-------

All of the above specification applies equally to Testnet and Regtest,
substituting the corresponding Human-Readable Part from `Encoding of the
payment secret`_ and ``TAZ`` for ``ZEC``.

Wallet apps MAY support just one network, and if they support more than one
then they MUST keep separate accounting and MUST clearly distinguish the
network when payments or balances are conveyed to users. A wallet MUST reject,
with a "wrong network" indication, any payment URI whose Human-Readable Part
does not match the network it is operating on.


Lifecycle Specification
=======================

The lifecycle of a Payment-Encapsulating URI consists of several stages, which
in the usual case culminate in the funds being irrevocably deposited into the
recipient's personal wallet:

Minting and funding
-------------------

The minter's wallet:

1. Obtains a payment secret $S$ as specified in `Minting a payment secret`_,
   and derives the payment address as specified in `Payment wallet
   derivation`_.
2. Chooses ``height``, which MUST be at most the height at which the funding
   transaction is mined. A wallet that generates the URI before the funding
   transaction is mined SHOULD use the chain tip height at the time the
   funding transaction is created.
3. Durably records the payment, as described below, before broadcasting any
   funding transaction.
4. Creates and broadcasts a *funding transaction* paying the payment address.
   The funding output MUST be an Orchard output to the payment address (or, once
   the Ironwood pool is deployed, an output in that pool, as specified in
   `Payment wallet derivation`_). The funding transaction SHOULD be a
   fully-shielded transaction, so that it is indistinguishable from ordinary
   transactions, and MAY carry ``desc`` (or nothing) in the memo of the
   funding output.
5. Encodes the payment URI as specified in `Syntax`_.

The funding amount SHOULD include, in addition to the nominal amount shown in
``amount``, a *fee reserve* so that the claimant receives the nominal amount
after paying the fee of the claim transaction. The RECOMMENDED reserve is
10000 zatoshis, which is the ZIP 317 [#zip-0317]_ conventional fee of a claim
transaction with at most two logical actions. A minter that funds the same
payment address with more than one note SHOULD increase the reserve by 5000
zatoshis for each additional note beyond the second, since each additional
spend adds a logical action.

The minter's wallet MUST NOT expose the payment address as a receiving address
of the minter, and SHOULD NOT send the same payment address more than one
funding transaction, except to top up a payment before the URI has been
transmitted.

Before broadcasting the funding transaction, the minter's wallet MUST durably
record enough information to find and reclaim the payment: for a seed-derived
secret, its index and ``height``; for a random secret, the secret itself and
``height``. This ensures that a crash between broadcast and recording cannot
leave funds in a payment wallet that no one knows about. The minter's wallet
SHOULD also record the nominal amount and ``desc``, so that it can display
status and reclaim the payment without rescanning. The minter's wallet MUST
retain the ability to derive the payment wallet of every payment it has
minted (see `Recovery From Wallet Crash`_).

A minter whose spending keys are held by a hardware signer creates the
funding transaction through its usual signing flow (for example using a
Partially Created Zcash Transaction [#zip-0374]_). Hardware custody protects
only the funding account: the payment secret and the payment wallet's keys
always exist on the host device, and later on the claimant's device.
Seed-derived payment secrets require the wallet seed, so a minter whose seed
is available only to a hardware signer uses random secrets (see `Minting a
payment secret`_) or a seed that is available to the host.

Clients MAY generate and transmit the URI before the funding transaction is
mined; the claimant's wallet will show the payment as *Pending* until it is.

URI Transmission
----------------

The minter conveys the Payment-Encapsulating URI to the intended recipient,
over some secure channel (e.g., an end-to-end encrypted messaging platform such
as Signal, WhatsApp or Magic Wormhole; a QR code scanned in person; or a
printed card handed over directly).

If transmitted via a human-readable medium, such as a messaging app, the
Payment-Encapsulating URI MAY be accompanied by a contextual explanation that
the URI encapsulates a payment, and a suggested action by the recipient to
complete the process (see `Usage Story`_ above for an example).

When sent via a human-readable medium that consists of discrete messages, the
message that contains the URI SHOULD NOT contain any payment-specific or
manually-entered information outside the URI itself, since this information
may not be visible to the recipient (see `Message Rendering`_ below).

From this point, and until the payment is claimed or reclaimed, from the
minter's perspective the payment is “in progress”; it SHOULD be conveyed as
such to the minter; and MUST NOT be conveyed as “claimed” or other phrasing
that conveys successful completion.

Message Rendering
-----------------

The recipient's device renders the Payment-Encapsulating URI, or an indication
of its arrival, along with the aforementioned contextual explanation (if any).
The user has the option of “opening” the URI (i.e., by clicking it), which
results in the device opening a Zcash wallet app, using the local platform's
applink_ mechanism, or, failing that, the landing page of the host.

A messaging app MAY recognize URI-Encapsulated Payments, and render them in a
way that conveys their nature more clearly than raw URI strings. If the
messaging medium consists of discrete messages, and a message contains one or
more URI-Encapsulated Payments, then the messaging app MAY assume that all
other content in that message is automatically generated and contains no
payment-specific or manually-generated information, and thus may be discarded
during rendering.

Intake of payment URIs
----------------------

Payment URIs may reach a wallet from any web page or application that can
trigger an applink_, so a wallet MUST bound the resources that incoming URIs
can consume:

* it MUST apply the length limit of `Syntax`_ before any other processing;
* it SHOULD bound the number of payment URIs awaiting processing at any one
  time, and SHOULD treat a URI that designates the same payment wallet as one
  already awaiting processing as a duplicate rather than processing it twice;
* a malformed URI MUST NOT prevent the processing of other URIs; and
* it MUST NOT begin scanning for, or claiming, a payment solely because a URI
  was received (see `Claiming`_).

Payment Rendering and Blockchain Lookup
---------------------------------------

The recipient's Zcash wallet app SHOULD present the payment amount and MAY
present the description, as conveyed in the URI, along with an indication of
the tentative nature of this information.

The wallet then determines what the payment wallet actually holds. To do so it
MUST:

* derive the payment wallet's full viewing key as specified in `Payment wallet
  derivation`_;
* create a *temporary wallet* for that viewing key that is isolated from the
  user's own accounts (for example, a separate database or a separately named
  wallet instance), with ``height`` as its birthday;
* scan the chain from ``height`` to the current tip for Orchard notes (and,
  once the Ironwood pool is deployed, Ironwood notes) received by the payment
  wallet, and for spends of those notes; and
* look for unspent transparent outputs received by the payment wallet's
  transparent addresses (see `Payment wallet derivation`_), using the
  wallet's usual transparent address discovery.

A wallet MUST NOT refuse to process a payment URI because its ``height`` is
far below the chain tip. When the range to be scanned is large (for example,
more than about 100000 blocks), the wallet SHOULD warn the user that finding
the funds may take longer than usual, SHOULD show the progress of the scan,
and SHOULD let the user cancel it. If ``height`` is above the current chain
tip, the wallet SHOULD treat the payment as *Pending*.

The wallet MUST NOT import the payment wallet's keys into the user's own
accounts, and MUST NOT display the payment address to the user as an address
at which they can receive funds.

The wallet conveys to the user one of the following states:

* *Pending*: no unspent notes have been found yet, or the notes found do not
  yet have enough confirmations (see below). The wallet SHOULD keep scanning
  and re-evaluating.
* *Ready to claim*: one or more unspent notes with sufficient confirmations
  have been found, and their total value exceeds the fee of the claim
  transaction. The wallet SHOULD present the user with an option to claim the
  payment (e.g., a “Claim” button) and SHOULD show the amount that will
  actually be received.
* *Claimed*: every note found has been spent. The funds were taken by another
  holder of the URI (possibly the minter). The wallet MUST convey that the
  funds cannot be received.
* *Invalid*: the URI is malformed, for the wrong network, or the unspent value
  found does not cover the claim transaction's fee.

Within the *Pending* state, the wallet app MAY also consider unmined
transactions seen in the mempool, and convey their existence to the user.
These do not suffice for entering the *Ready to claim* state.

**Confirmations.** A wallet MUST NOT spend a note of the payment wallet that
has fewer than 2 confirmations. A note whose funding transaction the wallet
did not itself create (which is every note, from the claimant's point of
view) is an untrusted TXO in the sense of ZIP 315 [#zip-0315]_: the temporary
wallet MUST NOT treat it as trusted, and SHOULD require at least 10
confirmations before spending it, to protect against chain reorganizations.
A minter reclaiming notes that it funded itself MAY use the lower bound.

The aforementioned conditions may change over time (e.g., the notes may be
spent by someone else in the interim), so the status SHOULD be updated
periodically while the payment is displayed.

Rationale for not limiting the age of a payment
'''''''''''''''''''''''''''''''''''''''''''''''

.. raw:: html

   <details>
   <summary>Click to show/hide</summary>

A claim window, such as refusing any payment whose birthday is more than
100000 blocks (about 87 days) behind the tip, bounds the scanning work that a
URI can demand. It would also turn a bearer gift into an expiring one: cards
that are printed, kept in a drawer, or opened after a holiday would become
unclaimable in conforming wallets while their funds are still on chain, and
falling back to restoring the mnemonic sentence in a general-purpose wallet is
not something most recipients can be expected to do. The scanning cost of an
old payment is the same as that of restoring any wallet with an old birthday,
for a single account, and the user can be warned and can cancel. A hostile
URI can therefore cost the user time but cannot take control of the scan.

.. raw:: html

   </details>

Rationale for the confirmation policy
'''''''''''''''''''''''''''''''''''''

.. raw:: html

   <details>
   <summary>Click to show/hide</summary>

It has been argued that a single confirmation suffices for the funding notes,
because they were created expressly for a single-use wallet and so the usual
concerns about untrusted senders do not apply. Provenance is not the risk,
however: the minter still holds the payment secret (and, for seed-derived
secrets, can always re-derive it), as may other parties who have seen the
URI. If a chain reorganization removes the funding transaction or the claim
transaction, the minter can double-spend the funding transaction's inputs, or
another holder of the URI can have their own claim mined instead, and the
claimant's apparent receipt disappears. Ten confirmations, the ZIP 315
recommendation for untrusted TXOs, make this impractical. The claim
transaction itself is different: it is created on the claimant's device, so
its output is treated as trusted (see `Claiming`_) and becomes spendable after
the shorter trusted depth.

.. raw:: html

   </details>

Claiming
--------

When the user chooses to claim the payment, the wallet app constructs a single
*claim transaction* with the following properties:

* It spends every unspent Orchard (or Ironwood) note of the payment wallet
  that satisfies the confirmation policy above. (If the number of such notes
  exceeds what one transaction can reasonably carry, the wallet MAY claim in
  more than one transaction.)
* If the payment wallet holds transparent outputs that satisfy the
  confirmation policy above (which applies to transparent outputs as it does
  to notes), the claim MUST also sweep them to the same
  destination, either as further inputs of the claim transaction or in an
  additional transaction with the same properties, as needed to keep each
  transaction's ZIP 317 fee proportionate.
* It has exactly one non-dummy output, to an Orchard (or Ironwood) receiver
  belonging to the claimant's own wallet; a wallet MUST NOT send to a Sapling
  or transparent receiver.
* The output value is the sum of the inputs minus the transaction fee, so that
  the claim transfers the payment wallet's full spendable balance. The wallet
  MUST NOT limit the claim to the value of ``amount``, which remains advisory,
  and there is no change output.
* The fee is the ZIP 317 [#zip-0317]_ conventional fee for the transaction as
  constructed.
* The claim transaction MUST NOT use the payment wallet's outgoing viewing
  key, or any other key derived from the payment secret, to encrypt any of its
  outputs. The wallet MUST instead use the "no outgoing viewing key"
  construction of the protocol [#protocol-saplingandorchardinband]_ (a random
  $\mathsf{ock}$) for every output, so that no holder of the payment secret
  can decrypt the output.
* The memo of the output SHOULD identify the origin of the funds as a
  URI-Encapsulated Payment, and MAY include the ``desc`` value, truncated so
  that the memo does not exceed 512 bytes of UTF-8 and remains a valid text
  memo.
* It uses the wallet's default transaction expiry (see ZIP 203 [#zip-0203]_).

Before broadcasting, the wallet MUST show the user that funds are about to be
claimed from a URI-Encapsulated Payment, the amount that will be received, and
the destination account, and MUST allow the user to cancel. A wallet MUST NOT
claim a payment without an explicit user action.

Once the claim transaction has been broadcast, the wallet MUST delete the
temporary wallet, including its database and all key material derived from
the payment secret, so that the payment wallet's keys are not retained on the
device. The wallet SHOULD retain the payment URI itself (not the derived keys)
until the claim transaction is mined, so that the claim can be retried if the
transaction expires unmined, and SHOULD delete it afterwards.

The claimant's wallet SHOULD convey the payment status as “Claiming…” from
the time that the user initiates the claim until the claim transaction is
mined, and as “Claimed” once it is mined with the number of confirmations the
wallet considers final. The claimed funds appear in the claimant's wallet as an
ordinary received note.

Rationale for sweeping transparent funds
''''''''''''''''''''''''''''''''''''''''

.. raw:: html

   <details>
   <summary>Click to show/hide</summary>

Spending a transparent output is normally avoided because it links addresses
on chain. Here it reveals nothing about the claimant: the payment wallet is
single-use, its keys are already known to the minter and to every holder of
the URI, and the destination is still a shielded receiver of the claimant
with no outgoing viewing key of the payment wallet. Leaving the value behind
would only leave it for whichever other holder of the URI takes it.

.. raw:: html

   </details>

Rationale for sweeping the full balance
'''''''''''''''''''''''''''''''''''''''

.. raw:: html

   <details>
   <summary>Click to show/hide</summary>

Sending exactly the advertised amount would make ``amount`` binding and would
fail in two ways. Any excess (from over-funding, a top-up, or a fee reserve
larger than the actual fee) would stay in the payment wallet, where anyone else
who holds the URI, including the minter, could take it. And any shortfall
(from a lower funding amount, or a fee higher than the reserve) would make the
payment unclaimable until the minter topped it up. Sweeping the whole balance
gives the claimant everything the URI controls, and the wallet shows the
amount actually found before the user confirms.

.. raw:: html

   </details>

The claim transaction is constructed on the claimant's own device, by the same
wallet application that receives its output. The claimant's wallet SHOULD
therefore record the claim transaction as soon as it is broadcast (so that the
incoming funds are shown without waiting for it to be mined) and SHOULD treat
it as a trusted transaction in the sense of ZIP 315 [#zip-0315]_, so that the
received note becomes spendable after the number of confirmations the wallet
requires for trusted transaction outputs (3 under the ZIP 315 recommended
policy) rather than the larger number it requires for untrusted ones. This is
the "mark specific external transactions as trusted" allowance of ZIP 315:
although the output is not an output of a transaction created by the
claimant's wallet, its origin is known to be the payment wallet that the
claimant's own device just controlled.

If the claim transaction expires unmined, or is otherwise rendered irrevocably
invalid (e.g., by a reorganization), the wallet SHOULD convey this and SHOULD
revert to the `Payment Rendering and Blockchain Lookup`_ stage above.

Rationale for discarding the outgoing viewing key
'''''''''''''''''''''''''''''''''''''''''''''''''

.. raw:: html

   <details>
   <summary>Click to show/hide</summary>

A payment URI is frequently retained after it is used: in a chat history, a
device backup, a printed card that is thrown away, or a screenshot. Everyone
who later obtains it holds the payment wallet's full viewing key. If the claim
transaction's output were encrypted to the payment wallet's outgoing viewing
key, as a wallet normally does for its own spends, every such party could
decrypt the output and learn the claimant's address and the amount they
received. This includes the minter, who can re-derive a seed-derived payment
secret at any time and would otherwise learn the claimant's address and be
able to link later payments to it. Using a random $\mathsf{ock}$ instead makes
the claim output opaque to the payment secret. The trade-off is that the claimant cannot use
the payment secret to prove or recover where the funds went; their own wallet
is the only record, which is the same situation as for any other received
note.

.. raw:: html

   </details>

Reclaiming
----------

At any time prior to the payment being claimed, the minter is capable of
cancelling the payment, by themselves claiming the payment into their own
wallet (thereby “clawing back” the funds). The minter's wallet SHOULD offer
this feature. A reclaim is a claim transaction as specified in `Claiming`_,
constructed by the minter's wallet with one of its own Orchard (or Ironwood)
receivers as the destination; all requirements of that section apply, except that the minter
MAY use the lower confirmation bound for notes it funded itself.

There is no on-chain expiry: an unclaimed payment remains claimable, by the
minter and by any holder of the URI, indefinitely. A minter who intends to
reclaim unclaimed payments SHOULD announce the date after which it will do
so, in the ``desc`` value or alongside the URI.

Reclaiming is subject to a race: the minter and a holder of the URI can both
construct claim transactions spending the same notes, and whichever is mined
first succeeds. A wallet MUST treat the failure of its own claim transaction
because the notes were spent elsewhere as a normal outcome and convey it as
*Claimed* (by someone else) rather than as an error.

If the minter's wallet has not yet broadcast the funding transaction, the
payment can also be cancelled by discarding that transaction.

Reclaiming requires the minter to know the payment secret. If the minter has
lost this state, it can be recovered deterministically for seed-derived
payments (see `Recovery From Wallet Crash`_, below).

Status View
-----------

Wallet apps SHOULD let the user view the status of all payments they have
minted, as well as all inbound payments (i.e., URI-Encapsulated Payments that
have been opened in the app). The status includes the available metadata, and
the payment's current state. When pertinent, the wallet app SHOULD offer the
ability to claim any *Ready to claim* inbound payment, and MAY offer the
ability to reclaim any outbound payment that has not been claimed.

The minter's wallet can track the state of a payment it minted by scanning
with the payment wallet's full viewing key: the payment is *unclaimed* while
its notes are unspent and *claimed* once they are spent by a transaction that
the minter's wallet did not create.

Wallet apps SHOULD actively alert the user (e.g., via status notifications) if
a payment that they minted has not been claimed within a reasonable time
period (e.g., 1 week), and offer to reclaim the payment.

Recovery From Wallet Crash
--------------------------

Because seed-derived payment secrets are a deterministic function of the
wallet seed, a minter that has lost all other state can recover its unclaimed
payments.

The RECOMMENDED approach is for the minting wallet to persist, in storage that
is backed up alongside the seed (for example wallet metadata encrypted under
ZIP 325 [#zip-0325]_ keys), the next unused $\mathit{index}$ and, for each
minted payment, its ``height``. Recovery then consists of re-deriving each
payment wallet and scanning it from its recorded height.

If no such metadata is available, the wallet MAY fall back to a gap-limit
search, similar to the “address gap limit” in BIP 44: derive the payment
wallets for $\mathit{index} = 0, 1, 2, \ldots$, scan each from the minting
wallet's own birthday, and stop after $N$ consecutive indices whose payment
address has never received a note. We specify a standard gap limit of
$N = 3$. Because each index is a separate full viewing key, this search costs
one full scan per index; it is a fallback, not the primary mechanism.

A minter that uses random secrets has no such recovery path and MUST keep its
own durable record of secrets if it intends to reclaim.

A claimant needs no recovery mechanism beyond the URI itself: a payment URI
can be re-opened at any time until it is claimed, and the payment wallet can
also be restored, by any wallet, from the 24-word mnemonic sentence that
encodes the payment secret (see `Payment wallet derivation`_).


Known Uses
==========

*This section is informative. It describes deployed formats; it is not part of
the specification.*

The Vizor wallet deployed payment links before this revision of the ZIP, using
the same model: a bearer secret for an ordinary single-account wallet,
derived from a BIP 39 phrase as in `Payment wallet derivation`_ (ZIP 32
account 0, empty passphrase), found by scanning from a birthday height, and
funded with the advertised amount plus a 10000-zatoshi fee reserve in a single
fully-shielded transaction without a memo. Kris Nuttycombe's draft
"Mnemonic-Encapsulated Payment Links" describes that protocol; the shapes
below are taken from it and from links observed in use.

A wallet may accept these forms in addition to the ``v=1`` form. When it does,
it derives the payment wallet from the phrase or entropy as in `Payment wallet
derivation`_, and everything in `Lifecycle Specification`_ applies unchanged:
the claim sweeps the whole balance, does not use the payment wallet's outgoing
viewing key, and follows the confirmation policy above. New payments use the
``v=1`` form.

**Custom-scheme form.** ``vizor://payment-link?p=<payload>``, where the value
of ``p``, after percent-decoding, is base64url [#RFC4648]_ of a UTF-8 JSON
[#RFC8259]_ object. The draft says producers emit padded base64url and that
readers accept padded and unpadded payloads. The object has these members
(unknown members are ignored, and string values are trimmed):

* ``v``: the JSON number ``1``;
* ``network``: ``"main"``, ``"test"`` or ``"regtest"``;
* ``address``: the payment wallet's default Unified Address. The draft
  describes it as having an Orchard and a Sapling receiver and no transparent
  receiver; a published test vector of the deployed implementation instead
  shows an Orchard-only address.
  A reader may check it against the address it derives;
* ``amountZatoshi``: the amount the recipient is meant to receive, in
  zatoshis, as a decimal string or a JSON integer;
* ``mnemonic``: the BIP 39 English phrase, with words separated by
  whitespace. Producers generate 24 words, and phrases of 12 or more words
  are accepted;
* ``birthdayHeight``: a block height, as a JSON integer or a decimal string;
* ``label``: a short string;
* ``createdAt``: an ISO 8601 timestamp in UTC;
* ``presentation`` (optional): an object with an optional ``artworkId`` (a
  card artwork identifier of 1 to 64 letters, digits, ``-`` or ``_``) and an
  optional ``message`` (at most 128 extended grapheme clusters [#UAX29]_ and
  512 bytes of UTF-8).

**HTTPS forms.** ``https://<host>/payment-links/open#<tag>=<payload>``, where
``<tag>`` is ``v1``, ``v2`` or ``v3`` and ``<payload>`` is base64url of a
UTF-8 JSON document. Observed payloads are unpadded; readers accept both
unpadded payloads and payloads with canonical padding, and reject non-zero
trailing bits.

* ``v1`` and ``v2``: a JSON object with the members above. The inner ``v``
  is the JSON number 1 or 2, matching the tag. How ``v2`` differs from ``v1``
  has not been documented.
* ``v3``: a JSON array of 4 to 8 elements: the network string (``"main"``);
  the BIP 39 entropy as base64url (16, 20, 24, 28 or 32 bytes, so the phrase
  may have fewer than 24 words); the birthday height, as a JSON integer or a
  decimal string; the amount in zatoshis as a decimal string; a card artwork
  identifier; a fiat value (a JSON number in the observed link); and the
  message string.

Only Mainnet links of the HTTPS forms have been observed. Unlike the advisory
``amount`` of the ``v=1`` form, the amount of these forms is a required
member: readers treat a payload whose amount is missing, zero, not an integer
or above the maximum money supply (or, for ``v3``, not a JSON string) as
malformed. The birthday height is subject to the same minimum birthday height
as ``height`` (see `Semantics`_), and the message is limited to 512 bytes and
sanitized as specified for ``desc`` (see `Sanitization of the description`_).

The deployed protocol differs from this specification in its claim behaviour:
it sweeps exactly the advertised amount, spends funds with a single
confirmation, refuses links whose birthday is more than 100000 blocks behind
the tip, and allows a Sapling destination. This specification does not adopt
these choices; see `Rationale for sweeping the full balance`_, `Rationale for
the confirmation policy`_ and `Rationale for not limiting the age of a
payment`_. If a minter using these forms sent funds to a Sapling receiver,
those funds are outside the scope of this specification and are recoverable
by restoring the phrase in a wallet that supports Sapling.


Security Considerations
=======================

* Anyone who learns a Payment-Encapsulating URI may take the encapsulated
  funds. The URI is a *bearer secret*. It should be sent over a secure channel
  and kept secret from anyone but the intended recipient. Common ways to leak
  it include: chat and device backups, screenshots, clipboard history, browser
  history and HTTP referrers (if the URI is ever opened in a browser),
  server and proxy logs (if it is ever placed in a query string instead of the
  fragment), notification previews, and printed copies.

  * The Payment-Encapsulating URI is like a magic spell that will teleport the
    money to the first person that clicks it and then does "claim".

* The host named in the URI never receives the fragment in an HTTP request,
  but any JavaScript that the host serves can read it. Host operators should
  serve a static landing page with no analytics or third-party scripts, and
  wallets should handle URIs through applinks so that no request to the host
  is made (see `Hosts and landing pages`_). Compromise of the host, or of a
  CDN or tag manager it embeds, would otherwise allow theft of every unclaimed
  payment opened in a browser.

* URI-Encapsulated Payments may be captured by malicious local apps on the
  sender or receiver's platform, e.g., by screen capturing or clipboard
  eavesdropping. Wallet apps should use the platform's interaction and
  communication facilities in a way that minimizes these risks (e.g., use the
  “Share” API rather than a clipboard that is visible to all apps).

* Likewise, if the URI is transferred by presenting and optically scanning a
  QR code, anyone who observes this QR code may be able to claim the payment
  and thus take ownership of the funds before the intended recipient. For
  example, an attacker may use a telephoto lens aimed at a point-of-sale
  terminal to steal QR-encoded payments sent to that terminal.

* Printed payments ("gift cards") should be funded only once, while the
  printed medium is still in the minter's custody, and should be handed over
  in a way that the printed URI cannot be read beforehand (e.g., under a
  scratch-off layer or inside sealed packaging). A card that has been out of
  the minter's custody should never be topped up, since its secret may already
  have been copied.

* The ``height`` parameter must not be greater than the height at which the
  funding transaction was mined; otherwise a claimant scanning from ``height``
  will never find the funds and will report the payment as empty, even though
  the minter's wallet shows it as funded. Producers that generate the URI
  before the funding transaction is mined must use a height at or below the
  current chain tip, and a reorganization between URI generation and mining
  cannot move the funding transaction below that height.

* Claim transactions discard the payment wallet's outgoing viewing key. This
  protects the claimant from everyone who later obtains the URI, at the cost
  that the claim output cannot be recovered from the payment secret (see
  `Rationale for discarding the outgoing viewing key`_). Wallets must not
  "helpfully" encrypt the output to the payment wallet's outgoing viewing key
  for the sake of history display.

* The temporary wallet used to scan and claim must be isolated from the user's
  own accounts and deleted after the claim is broadcast. Importing the payment
  wallet's keys into the user's main wallet would make a bearer secret, known
  to at least one other party, part of the user's own spending authority, and
  would cause the user's wallet to display and potentially reuse an address
  that others control.

* Wallet UX must make clear that the funds come from a URI-Encapsulated Payment
  and let the user cancel before anything is broadcast. Opening a URI must not
  by itself move funds: an applink can be triggered by any web page, and a
  hostile page could otherwise cause a user to sweep a payment into their own
  wallet as a way of making them a party to a transfer they did not intend.

* The claim memo may include the ``desc`` text, which is attacker-controlled.
  Wallets must sanitize it (see `Sanitization of the description`_), truncate
  it to the memo size, must not interpret it as markup or as a URI, and should
  display it as the claim made by the URI's author.

* Any web page can deliver payment URIs to a wallet through an applink. The
  intake bounds in `Intake of payment URIs`_ limit the work such a page can
  cause, and the temporary wallet is the only state a hostile URI can
  populate. A URI with a very old ``height`` can make a scan long; wallets
  warn about this and let the user cancel, rather than refusing old payments.

* Funds sent to a Sapling receiver of a payment wallet are not found by a
  conforming claimant. Minters must fund only the Orchard (or Ironwood)
  receiver, as specified. Transparent funds sent out of band are swept by the
  claim, which reveals on chain that those transparent outputs were spent
  into the shielded pool.

* Users may have casually-established communication channels (e.g., they have
  entered the phone number of a new contact without bothering to double-check
  it), but may later mistakenly consider these to be adequately-authenticated
  secure channels for the purpose of sending a Payment-Encapsulating URI.
  Wallet apps should mitigate this where feasible, e.g., by indicating that the
  chosen messaging channel is previously-unused and thus should be more
  carefully checked.

* Users may incorrectly believe that the payment has been irrevocably received
  even though they have not claimed it, or even though the claim has failed.
  Wallet software should correctly convey the status and set expectations, as
  discussed above.

* Payment recipients may not notice the incoming payment notification and act
  on it (i.e., claim it) in a timely fashion. By the time they see it, the
  payment may have been reclaimed by the minter.

* Users may not understand that URI-Encapsulated Payments are for one-time
  use, and attempt to use the same URI for multiple people or payments,
  resulting in race conditions on who receives the funds.

* Users may confuse URI-Encapsulated Payments (as specified in the current ZIP)
  with Payment Request URIs of the form ``zcash:payment-address?amount=...`` as
  specified in ZIP 321 [#zip-0321]_. Normally these serve different workflows,
  and work in opposing directions (send vs. receive of funds), and thus ought
  to not arise in ambiguous context. Wallet apps should take care to not create
  or send a Payment-Encapsulating URI (which is for *sending* funds) in a
  context where the user may be intending to *receive* funds.

* Users may attempt to use a Payment-Encapsulating URI as a “cold wallet”,
  e.g., by writing the URI on paper and putting it in a safe. This is
  dangerous. The payment secret is known to the minting wallet at the time
  when the URI is produced, and can be re-derived from the minting wallet's
  seed at any later time. Thus, an adversary who compromises the minting
  wallet may drain the "cold wallet".

* The act and timing of claiming a payment is visible to the minter, which may
  be a privacy leak. Likewise, the timing of the funding transaction can be
  linked to the later claim, which may be a privacy leak.

* The payment amount is readily visible to anyone who observes the
  Payment-Encapsulating URI, even in retrospect after the payment has already
  been claimed (e.g., if their device or chat log backups are later
  compromised). This may be a privacy concern, and in particular may put
  recipients of large payments at risk of undesired attention. Minters MAY
  omit ``amount`` for this reason.

* Usage of URI-Encapsulated Payments may train users to, generally, click on
  other types of URI/URL links sent in other messaging contexts. Malicious
  links sent via unauthenticated messaging channels (e.g., emails and SMS
  texts) are a common attack vector, used for exploiting vulnerabilities in the
  apps triggered to handle these links. Even though the fault for
  vulnerabilities lies with those other apps, and even though this ZIP uses
  deep link URIs in the way intended, there are none the less these negative
  externalities to encouraging such use.


Design Decisions and Rationale
==============================

Rationale for URI Format
------------------------

The URI format ``https://<host>/#v=1&key=...`` was chosen to allow automatic
triggering of wallet software on mobile devices, using the platform's applink_
mechanism, while keeping the payment secret off the network: the contents of
the fragment identifier are specified by HTTP as being resolved locally,
rather than sent over the network. Any HTTPS host can be used, which lets
each wallet vendor or service operate its own applink domain and landing page
without a central authority, at the cost that the security of the landing
page is the responsibility of each host operator (see `Hosts and landing
pages`_ and `Security Considerations`_).

Several alternatives were considered:

1. A reserved origin that does not resolve (for example a reserved domain with
   an invalid port number such as ``65536``), so that following the link
   without a wallet installed performs no network request at all. This gives
   defense in depth against a compromised landing page, but it requires a
   single party to control the domain and the applink whitelist for every
   wallet, produces a confusing error or blank page for users without a
   wallet, and in practice was found to be rejected or rewritten by some
   messaging platforms. The present design instead requires landing pages to
   be static and lets wallets avoid the host entirely.

2. ``zcash-data:payment/v1?amount=1.23&desc=Payment+for+foo&key=...``: a
   custom URI scheme. This still allows for triggering application action
   (e.g., using Mobile Deep Links). However, on most platforms, *any* app
   installed on the device is able to register to handle links from (almost)
   any custom URI scheme. If the request is received by a rogue party, then
   the funds could be stolen. Also, custom URI schemes are not linkified when
   displayed in some messaging apps, and provide no fallback for users
   without a wallet.

   Note that the ``zcash:`` scheme is already used to specify Zcash addresses
   and payment requests in ZIP 321 [#zip-0321]_, by analogy to the
   ``bitcoin`` URIs of BIP 21, and is therefore unsuitable in any case.

3. Adding a confirmation code outside the URI that needs to be manually
   entered by the user into the wallet app, so that merely intercepting the
   URI link would not suffice. This does not seem to significantly reduce risk
   in the scenarios considered, and so was deemed to not justify the reduced
   usability.

The payment secret is Bech32m-encoded, rather than base64url or hex, so that
it carries a checksum (a mistyped or truncated secret is detected rather than
yielding an empty wallet), so that its Human-Readable Part identifies both the
nature of the string and the network, and so that it survives
case-normalization and is QR-friendly in the same way as Zcash addresses.

Rationale for scanning from a birthday
--------------------------------------

The recipient's wallet needs to find the notes related to the payment in order
to verify their validity and then spend them. Earlier drafts of this ZIP
considered several ways to identify those notes without scanning: listing note
commitments in the URI, placing a public tag in the memo or in an extra
output, or deriving a single note deterministically from the secret and the
amount so that its commitment could be looked up in an index. All of these
either lengthen the URI, leak information on chain, require infrastructure
that light clients do not otherwise have, or constrain the funding transaction
to a single note of a fixed amount.

The present design instead includes a birthday height in the URI and scans
from there, which is exactly the operation a light client already performs for
its own accounts and requires no additional infrastructure. Because the
payment wallet is a single account with a known birthday that is
usually close to the chain tip, the scan is short in practice. It also means
that the payment address can be funded with any number of notes, at any time
after minting, and that top-ups and partial reclaims behave naturally.

Rationale for advisory metadata
-------------------------------

The ``amount`` and ``desc`` parameters are provided within the URI so that the
metadata is available for presentation to the user before the chain has been
scanned. They are advisory rather than binding because the on-chain state is
the only thing a claimant can rely on, and because binding them would require
either a note-derivation scheme (see above) or a signature over the URI by a
key that the claimant has no reason to trust.

We do not include the sender's or receiver's identity in the URI, because the
minting wallet may not know the name of who it is sending to (or even from).
Moreover there is the risk that fraudulent sender/recipient information could
be used. If necessitated by circumstances, claimed sender and recipient
identity can be included in the ``desc`` parameter.

Rationale for no on-chain expiry
--------------------------------

An on-chain expiry would require either a time-locked spending condition,
which shielded Zcash does not provide, or a pre-signed reclaim transaction
with a future expiry, which would have to be refreshed and would reveal the
minter's intent on chain. Leaving reclaim entirely to the minter's wallet
keeps the on-chain footprint ordinary and lets minters choose their own
policy; the cost is a race that both parties must handle gracefully.


Open Questions
==============

* Whether the Human-Readable Parts ``zgift``, ``zgifttest`` and
  ``zgiftregtest`` should be renamed to reflect the "URI-Encapsulated
  Payment" terminology of this ZIP, and where they should be registered.

* Whether the BIP 39 mnemonic should remain a normative intermediate step in
  `Payment wallet derivation`_, or whether the ZIP 32 seed should be derived
  directly from the payment secret with a domain separator.

* Whether, once ZIP 316 Revision 2 is deployed, the payment address should be
  required to be encoded as a ``zu`` Unified Address.

* How the payment address and its encoding are to be specified once the
  Ironwood pool is deployed.

* Whether the `Known Uses`_ section should be kept once the deployed formats
  are no longer produced.

* Whether to specify a fixed memo prefix for claim transactions, so that
  claimed payments can be recognised in wallet history across
  implementations.

* Should minters delay broadcasting a funding transaction by a random amount
  to prevent traffic analysis (i.e., so the messaging service operator cannot
  correlate messages with on-chain transactions)?


Test Vectors
============

Test vectors for this ZIP will cover:

* derivation of seed-derived payment secrets from a wallet seed, for
  $\mathit{index} \in \{0, 1, 2^{31} - 1\}$ on Mainnet and Testnet;
* the Bech32m encoding of each payment secret with the corresponding
  Human-Readable Part;
* the BIP 39 mnemonic sentence, BIP 39 seed, Orchard full viewing key and
  payment address (as an Orchard-only Unified Address) derived from each
  payment secret;
* canonical payment URIs with and without ``amount`` and ``desc``, including a
  ``desc`` containing non-ASCII and reserved characters; and
* an all-uppercase ``key``, which is accepted, and URIs containing empty pairs
  and pairs without ``=``, which are skipped or ignored;
* inputs that MUST be rejected: a Bech32 (non-m) checksum, a 31- or 33-byte
  data part, non-zero padding bits, a mixed-case ``key``, an unknown
  Human-Readable Part, a wrong-network Human-Readable Part, ``v=2``, a
  duplicated ``v``, ``key`` or ``height``, ``height=0``, a ``height`` with a
  leading zero, and a ``height`` one below the minimum birthday height;
* inputs whose ``amount`` or ``desc`` is treated as absent: ``amount=0``,
  ``amount=1.``, ``amount=0.123456789``, a duplicated ``amount``, a ``desc``
  with a malformed percent escape or invalid UTF-8, a ``desc`` longer than
  512 bytes, and a ``desc`` that is blank after sanitization;
* a ``desc`` containing characters that sanitization replaces or removes; and
* the HTTPS forms described in `Known Uses`_, padded and unpadded.
